Your roster shows roughly 45 athletes on named check-in days, Monday through Friday, plus about 20 in the Bench group, and five more in Drive who are active in intake but not yet assigned a day. The system

Four moving parts, one of which is you

Collector

Nightly pull of organic performance from the Instagram Graph API and YouTube Data API into Supabase, which you already have connected. Backfills all 70 videos on first run.

Scorer

Ranks every post on engagement per impression, not raw views, so a strong recent post is not buried by a 2024 clip that had two years to accumulate.

Promoter

Turns an approved winner into a live Meta ad through the Marketing API: campaign, ad set, audience, creative, budget. Later, the same step against Google Ads for YouTube.

Governor

Auto-pauses any ad whose cost per quiz completion crosses your ceiling, enforces the daily cap, and files a weekly report. This half runs with no approval — stopping spend is always safe.

Where you sit in it: once a week you get a short list — “these three posts earned promotion, approve?” Launching costs money, so a human says yes. Pausing saves money, so the machine does it alone. That asymmetry is the whole safety design.

Money and measurement

What it costs and how we know it worked

Start at $15/day, about $450/month, for a 60-day read. That is enough volume to learn something and small enough that a bad month is not a real wound. Infrastructure is effectively free — Supabase and Zapier are already on your account.

Three metrics, in order. Do not skip ahead:

  • Cost per quiz completion — the only number that matters in month one. Below $4 and the machine is working.
  • Quiz → Learn to Lift conversion rate — measured in month two. If traffic is cheap but nobody buys, the problem is the nurture sequence, not the ads, and we fix that before adding budget.
  • Cost per sale against program price — the number that decides whether this scales past $450/month or gets switched off.

Honest risks, stated up front. Meta Business verification can take two weeks and is outside my control. Attribution on a quiz-to-email-to-purchase path is genuinely fuzzy, so treat month one as directional. And there is a real chance the answer is no — that paid traffic to a beginner program does not pay for itself at your price. A $900 two-month test is the cheapest way to find that out, and finding it out is a legitimate result.
