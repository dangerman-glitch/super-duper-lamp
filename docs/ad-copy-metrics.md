# Ad copy — "Sift through the endless metrics"

Seed creative for the $100 opening test. Top-ranked post in `ads.creative_scores`
(69.2 views/day, 25,593 lifetime — roughly 6x the next best).

- **Video:** https://youtu.be/OBu0HCABHe8
- **Audience:** cold, national, **excluding the Indianapolis metro**
- **Destination:** Find Your Phase quiz
- **Optimisation event:** `quiz_completed`
- **CTA button:** Learn more

Three primary-text variants. Run all three in the one cold ad set and let
Meta allocate — they test different angles on the same hook, not three
rewordings of one.

Meta truncates primary text around 125 characters on mobile before "See
more", so each first line carries the whole hook on its own.

---

## Variant A — the hook straight

Closest to what earned the views organically. Lowest-risk control.

> Most lifters are drowning in numbers that don't change a single decision.
>
> Bar speed. Tonnage. RPE. Intensity percentages. Peak power.
>
> Some of it tells you something. Most of it is noise you're tracking because
> it's there.
>
> The hard part was never collecting data. It's knowing which two or three
> numbers matter for where you are right now — and ignoring the rest without
> feeling like you're cutting corners.
>
> A lifter in their first year needs different information than someone eight
> weeks out from a meet. Same sport. Different signal.
>
> If you're not sure which phase you're in, sort that first. Everything else
> follows from it.
>
> Find your phase — link below. No pressure either way.

**Headline:** Which numbers actually matter
**Description:** Remote coaching for lifters who want a plan, not a spreadsheet

## Variant B — the phase angle

Strongest bridge to the quiz, because the quiz is genuinely the answer to the
question the copy raises rather than a CTA bolted onto an unrelated hook.

> Which numbers matter depends on where you are. Nobody tells you that part.
>
> Learning the lifts? Your signal is position and consistency. Bar velocity is
> a distraction.
>
> Building? Volume you can actually repeat, and whether last block's work
> stuck.
>
> Competing? Make-miss ratio under fatigue, and how you handle a bad opener.
>
> Same sport. Three completely different sets of useful information.
>
> Most lifters track all of it and act on none of it. The fix isn't more data.
> It's knowing which phase you're in.
>
> A few questions will tell you. Link below.

**Headline:** Find your phase
**Description:** Remote 1:1 weightlifting coaching. USAW Level 3 coach.

## Variant C — the cost angle

Names the fear, then reframes it. Highest-contrast of the three.

> You can spend a year tracking the wrong number.
>
> I've watched lifters log every set, every percentage, every bar speed
> reading, and still not know whether the block worked.
>
> That isn't a discipline problem. It's a signal problem. You were measuring
> things that don't answer the question you actually have.
>
> And the question is usually simpler than the spreadsheet: am I in the right
> phase, and is the work matching it?
>
> Sort that out and the metrics get obvious. Skip it and no amount of data
> helps.
>
> Find your phase — link below. No pressure either way.

**Headline:** Three questions. One answer.
**Description:** Remote coaching for lifters who want a plan, not a spreadsheet

## Notes

**No hashtags.** They belong on organic posts. In a paid placement they add
noise and leak taps away from the destination.

**Keep the original video unchanged.** It earned 25,593 views as-is. Do not
re-edit it, do not add a title card, do not append a CTA frame — the ad copy
and the button do that job. Changing the creative discards the one thing the
organic data actually validated.

**Captions on.** Most of this audience watches muted.

**Two things worth confirming before launch:**

1. **Does the quiz gate on email?** If it asks for an email before showing the
   result, say so in the copy — an unexpected gate is where cold traffic
   bounces, and the bounce shows up as a bad cost-per-quiz that looks like an
   ad problem.
2. **Is the "USAW Level 3" line in Variant B's description wanted?** The voice
   rule is that the reader is the hero and Dan is the guide. A credential in
   small print builds trust for a $200/mo service, but it is the one place in
   these drafts that points at Dan rather than the reader. Easy to drop.

**On the claims.** The metric examples (bar speed, tonnage, RPE, make-miss
ratio) are drawn from the source video's own subject. The phase-to-signal
mapping in Variant B is written to match the Learn / Improve / Compete
structure of the quiz — check it reads true against how the quiz actually
sorts people, since the ad promises what the quiz delivers.
