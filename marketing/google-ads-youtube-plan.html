Myriad Weightlifting · Google Ads & YouTube · 90-day plan

The First
Thirty Dollars

A $30/month ad budget can't buy clicks. It can buy an audience, a tracking system, and proof that your creative works — so that the day you can spend $400, it goes to work immediately instead of starting from zero.

01 The honest math

At $30/month, do not run Google Search ads. Run YouTube only.

Health & fitness keywords average about $5.00 per click on Google Search in 2026. Thirty dollars buys you six clicks a month — roughly one every five days. Google's bidding algorithm needs somewhere near 30 conversions in 30 days to leave its learning phase; six clicks will never produce one. You'd be paying $360 a year to teach the machine nothing.

YouTube is priced per view, not per click. At $0.03–$0.10 per view, the same $30 buys 300 to 1,000 people watching your face, all of whom land in a remarketing list you own forever. That list is the actual asset here.

Lifetime value, in one sentence: LTV is the total money one athlete pays you before they leave. If your average athlete pays $124/month and stays a year, that's $1,488 — and because you deliver it yourself through Sheets, nearly all of it is margin.

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is what you spend on ads to sign one athlete. Against a $1,488 LTV, spending even $300 to sign one athlete is a 5:1 return. Most businesses would take that trade all day.

Which means the thing stopping you isn't the economics. It's cash on hand. So the plan below is built to spend almost nothing while you prove the creative, then ramp hard once one athlete has paid for the next month of ads.

Caveat: these six are the active Stripe subscriptions. Athletes paying by invoice, cash, or through Myriad's gym billing won't appear here, so your real roster is likely larger and your real MRR higher. If so, every budget number below is conservative in your favor.

02 Week one — the free build

None of this costs money. All of it has to exist before a dollar is spent, because ads that can't be measured are just donations.

  1. Create the Google Ads account at ads.google.com. When it pushes you into "Smart Mode" during signup, look for the small Switch to Expert Mode link before you finish. Smart Mode hides the controls you need. If you get trapped, create the account without a campaign and it defaults to Expert.
  2. Link your YouTube channel (Dangerman OWL) under Tools → Linked accounts → YouTube. Without this link you cannot build audiences from people who watched your videos — the single most valuable free asset you have.
  3. Install the Google tag in the quiz app. Your quiz lives at dangerman-findyourphase.netlify.app — a Netlify single-page app, so the tag goes in the <head> of index.html in the project source, then redeploy. Pasting it into the Netlify dashboard won't work; it has to ship with the build.
  4. Make the result screen measurable — see the block below. This is the highest-value hour in this entire document, and on a single-page app it will not happen by itself.
  5. Create two conversion actions (Goals → Conversions → New), defined in the next block.
  6. Create the remarketing audiences (Tools → Audience manager): everyone who visited the quiz page, everyone who completed it, and — from the YouTube link — everyone who watched any Dangerman OWL video in the last 540 days.
  7. Turn off auto-applied recommendations. Recommendations → the three-dot menu → Auto-apply → uncheck everything. Google will otherwise "helpfully" broaden your targeting and eat a $30 budget in two days.

Making the Netlify quiz measurable

A single-page app renders the result screen without loading a new page, so the browser's address bar never changes and Google Ads never sees a "conversion." Nothing else in this plan works until this is fixed.

First — the 30-second test

Open the quiz, answer it to the end, and watch the address bar the moment your phase appears.

  • The URL changed (to something like /results) → you can use a simple destination-URL conversion. Still add the event below; it's more reliable.
  • The URL stayed on / → you have no conversion at all right now. The event below is mandatory.

Then — fire the event where the result renders

Find the component that displays the athlete's phase and add this where it first mounts. Get the conversion ID and label from Goals → Conversions → Quiz Complete → Tag setup → Install the tag yourself.

// index.html <head> — ships with the build, above your app bundle <script async src="https://www.googletagmanager.com/gtag/js?id=AW-XXXXXXXXX"></script> <script> window.dataLayer = window.dataLayer || []; function gtag(){dataLayer.push(arguments);} gtag('js', new Date()); gtag('config', 'AW-XXXXXXXXX'); </script> // In the results component — fires once, when the phase is shown useEffect(() => { window.gtag?.('event', 'conversion', { send_to: 'AW-XXXXXXXXX/YourLabelHere', value: 40.0, currency: 'USD' }); // gives the URL something Analytics and humans can read window.history.pushState({}, '', '/your-phase'); }, []);

If you add the pushState line, put a _redirects file in your public/ folder containing /* /index.html 200 — otherwise anyone who refreshes on that URL gets a Netlify 404.

Verify before spending a dollar. Install the Google Tag Assistant Chrome extension, run it on the quiz, complete the quiz, and confirm the conversion fires. Then check Goals → Conversions — status should move from "No recent conversions" to "Recording conversions" within about 24 hours. Do not launch until it says that.

One more thing worth doing on this domain

A bare .netlify.app address costs you clicks — it reads as unfinished to someone deciding whether to trust a coach with their training. Point a subdomain at it (Netlify does this free under Domain settings → Add custom domain): quiz.myriadweightlifting.com, or whatever domain you control. Do it before launch so the ads never carry the Netlify URL.

Turn on auto-tagging (Settings → Account settings → Auto-tagging) as well. The UTMs are for you reading a report; auto-tagging's gclid is what lets Google match a click back to a conversion.

Why "Video views" and not "Drive conversions": conversion-optimized video needs a volume of data you cannot buy for $30. The views campaign gets your face in front of the right people cheaply and fills the remarketing list. Conversions come later, from the list.

Why exclude TV screens: nobody takes a quiz on their living-room television. You'd pay for views that can't click.

Why the day-parting: your buyer is watching lifting content before training or after work. Restricting hours concentrates a tiny budget where intent is highest.

07 Ten minutes a week

Set a recurring Sunday block. Do only these four things. Resisting the urge to fiddle daily is most of the skill.

  1. Placement report. Content → Where ads showed. Exclude anything that spent over $1 with a view rate under 15%.
  2. View rate check. Above 25% is strong, 15–25% is fine, under 15% means the first five seconds aren't working — swap the ad, don't touch the targeting.
  3. Search terms (once Search is live). Keywords → Search terms. Add every irrelevant query as a negative. This is where most wasted spend hides.
  4. Log the numbers. Spend, views, quiz completions, consults booked. Four numbers in a sheet. Without the log you can't tell a rung is passed.

Kill criteria — decide these now, while it's cheap to be honest

  • Stop 60 days, $60 spent, zero quiz completions → the destination is broken, not the ads. Fix the quiz page before spending another dollar.
  • Hold View rate under 12% after two different ads → the offer isn't landing on cold traffic. Go back to organic content until something breaks out.
  • Go Cost per quiz completion under $60 → climb the ramp immediately, don't wait for the month to end.

08 Two things that beat the ad account

Said plainly, because $30/month deserves a straight answer about what it can and can't do.

Your organic YouTube is outperforming any budget you can currently fund. One video did 25,593 views. At the $0.04 CPV you'd bid, buying that reach costs about $1,024 — thirty-four months of your ad budget. Posting two more videos like it is a better use of a Saturday than any account optimization in this document. Use the ads to catch the people organic reach is already producing.

Google Ads has a free grant most solo coaches never claim. Google frequently offers new advertisers a spend-matching credit — typically "spend $500, get $500" over the first 60 days. It appears in the account after your first payment. You cannot use it at $30/month, but if a credit shows up in your account, that's the moment to pull the $400 rung forward: it's real money that expires, and it's the one case where spending ahead of the ramp is correct.
